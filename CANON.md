# PART A — PUBLIC CANON

## The heart (for framing, not quoting in-world)

- Heart Statement: *"If you had everything you ever wanted, would you still want the truth?"*
- The message: the cage is in the mind; being truly cheered on lets you become anything.

## The world in one breath

- In 2077 the world is one landmass made of three **Continuents** and ten **realms**. Oceans between them are gone; travel is easy; every culture is on everyone's doorstep.
- About 1.5 billion people live there, roughly 150 million per realm on average. Neuropa, the capital, may be larger.
- Terminology: the ten areas are **realms** ("the Realm of Neuropa"); the three groupings are **Continuents**. "Reaches" was rejected.
- **No sea today** (Katey, 3 Oct 2026): the continents squeezed together, so there are no open seas. The water by the Academy and Neuropa is a great man-made lake. Present-day copy says "lake"; oceans and seas appear only in the history of the Reconnecting. (The Bible still says "clifftops above the sea" in its Academy descriptions.)
- **Why "Continuent"** (Katey, 3 Oct 2026): every continent merged, so although it is now one landmass, the three groupings were renamed Continuents to honour them: continuations of the continents that came before, but new. The site explains this on Home (the story), the World page and the map.
- The main place in each realm simply takes the realm's name. Old cities survive as districts inside realms (Boston lives in Neuropa).
- Names have a cheerful, committee-made, "they tried to fix it" feel.

## The official story: the Great Reconnecting

- On **7 May 2027**, an undersea climate catastrophe beyond human control nearly ended everything. Humanity survived, the official story says, thanks to a divine fix. It is called **the Great Reconnecting**.
- It unfolded in three phases: **the Shake** (hours: quakes and tsunamis), **the Drift** (weeks to months: the continents grinding together) and **the Settling** (years: aftershocks, new land rising, coastlines holding).
- Around 80% of the world's population was lost, from over 8 billion to about 1.5 billion. Lost land is spread across every Continuent, so no single culture was erased.
- Most governments were lost. Survivors formed emergency coalitions by Continuent. Armies became the **Restoration Corp**, using military logistics for relief.
- **Nuclear weapons were dismantled in public ceremonies**: the Director's swords-into-ploughshares moment.
- Old nations survive as cultural identities, like districts within the realms.
- Faith grew, spirituality grew even more, and faiths became more open to one another.
- Everyone keeps their home language; bands translate in real time. The internet was rebuilt as the Academy's network. Neuropa produces most media, shown on public screens everywhere.
- The aftermath is remembered for its humanity: community, purpose, belonging and duty. "This time was a clean slate."
- Purple trees and bioluminescent landscapes appeared after the event. No one fully understands them: they are a research frontier and a wonder.

Mini versions of the Academy dot the realm, with its style and branding everywhere.

## The Academy

- **The Academy** is the seat of government, a giant town hall, and the world's favourite TV show: the broadcast is simply called *The Academy*. The name plays on everyone eternally growing, learning and evolving.
- **The building:** white marble and gold on clifftops above the sea; a vast glass-and-gold dome; slender towers; sweeping balconies; cypress trees and topiary; waterfalls; long reflecting pools; grand stairways; hovercraft drifting overhead. - **Symbol:** a gold, **eight-pointed compass star**, "for guidance". The Academy presents itself as the thing that points to truth. It appears on banners, robes and band engravings. (The house banners use a smaller **four-point star**.)
- **Robes:** black and white, embroidered with the gold compass.

### The School of Life (under 16)

- Schools became the School of Life. Points tasks help young people grow into functional adults. There is no single test: a whole childhood of behaviour, tracked by the band, shows what each child is naturally good at.

### The Academy year (at 16)

- Every 16-year-old spends one year at an Academy, like sixth form: one guaranteed year of structured education.
- Academy campuses exist in every realm, each mixing students from all ten realms, so every teenager spends a year away from home alongside people from everywhere.
- The whole year is broadcast, Big Brother style. Communities watch their own children and take part with points.
- No child knows their leaderboard score until they turn 16, like waiting for a letter.
- The **Compass Ceremony** opens the year. The **Exhibition** is its final exam.

### The Grand Academy and the Golden Prospects

- At 15, every child submits an entry to the wider network, judged and essentially crowdfunded with points by their community. Results are hidden. *(open: how this 15-year-old entry relates to the Exhibition submission at 16)*
- **Four students from every realm, 40 in total**, are chosen for the **Grand Academy** in Neuropa, the televised flagship. Selection rests purely on individual effort and standout community contribution; no gender rule. Everyone else attends a realm campus.
- The 40 are known as **the Golden Prospects**.
- At the Compass Ceremony the 40 are sorted into **four houses of ten**, each with one student from every realm. They rise or fall together.

### The Compass Ceremony

- Each student touches a **giant golden compass**. It spins and points to one of the four houses, each embodying one of the Academy's four rules.
- Two layers of identity: your realm is where you're from, carrying its North; your house is who you are.
- The fandom question: **"What's your house?"**

### The Exhibition and its season

- A grand, World's Fair-style event in marble pavilions, broadcast across all three Continuents, with pomp, music and a speech from the Director. Hunger Games-level spectacle where no one dies: the stakes are social and emotional. Inspired by the Director's love of the 1800s World's Fairs; the first was in 2037.
- Every 16-year-old submits something for growth and innovation in their passionate field. The official belief: the youngest minds are the most innovative and untainted. Submissions go into their realm's hub categories.
- Public slogan in the reference art: "Youth Innovation Shapes a Brighter Tomorrow." *(open: confirm as official)*
- **The season is the final eleven weeks of the Academy year:**
  1. **7 May, Reconnection Day:** five minutes of noise, then the season opens, honouring the rebuild heroes and the Seven.
  2. **Weeks 1–10:** one week per realm, with challenges themed on that realm's speciality, in the order drawn live by the Director at Thanksmas. In 2077, **Cariberia opens** and **Neuropa draws week 10**, the week before the final. *(open: weeks 2–9)*
  3. **Week 11: the Grand Final** at the Grand Academy in Neuropa, ending around **23 July** with **Placement Day**. Judging combines a panel of all ages, a public vote and each student's band profile.
- Every 16-year-old receives an offer: a path of training in a realm hub. They can accept or request rehoming. Those with no clear path are "invited" to Realignment.
- The very best become **Fellows of the Academy**: legends. A Fellow is the perfect blend of their realm's specialities and North and their house's rule.
- Scale: roughly 18 million 16-year-olds a year, nearly 2 million per realm, so the Exhibition needs tiers *(open)*.

## Life in Unitaria

### Basic rights and work

- Everything you deserve as a basic right is free: a home, good nutritious food, basic clothing, healthcare, learning and travel. Not everything is free, but every right is.
- Every career pays a basic living wage to everyone. Most people are placed in careers through the Exhibition.
- No one works a job for the points, so the real stakes are finding what you love.
- Bonus points come from excellent innovation and growth in your career, plus accolades.
- AI and robotics run the heavy background: energy, food production, water, transport maintenance and major construction. The Restoration Corp handles emergencies, hazards and dangerous work.

### Points

- Money no longer exists. Points are earned by completing community tasks. They are bonus fun: they pay for life upgrades and add-ons.
- **Weekly unlocks:** for example, 1,000 points opens a family's "Italian week" on the food menu instead of the standard food (good and nutritious, but always the same). Points also unlock home upgrades, on-trend developments and the latest clothes for your realm.
- Points buy commodities, virtual gifts for friends, access and community choices. Point-rich people can buy better seats and first picks.
- **Leaderboards** pop up through the band.
- **Pledging:** points can't buy a bigger say. People pledge points to vote on the community's predetermined needs, which also raises the points value of the related jobs. Pledges are capped per vote; the top 5 contributors each week get an extra 50 points to pledge.
- **No fines, no penalties.** Points are only ever lost by spending them. Do a bad job or don't show up, and you simply don't get the points.
- **Points expire yearly**, so no one can hoard. Trusts are exempt.
- **Starter trust at 21:** every adult has the right to one. The 21st birthday is an enormous milestone.
- **Most points funnel back into the community pot**, which each realm uses to make its Thanksmas as amazing as it likes.
- **Two ways to pay beyond tasks**, both pitched as kindness: trade in old-world belongings for points (branded as decluttering), or share your data ("your data is important, so you should be paid for it!").

| Age | Personal | To family | Trust |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 16 | 10% allowance for treats | 40% (discretionary for the family) | 50% to the Academy, paying for the Academy year, graduations, gowns and celebrations |
| 16–20 | 50%, no obligation to share | 0% | 50%, unlocked at 21 |
| 21 and over | 100%, full autonomy | Optional | None |

Every point a child earns also counts towards their hidden leaderboard total, revealed at 16.

### Task boards and the daily drops

1. The councils decide the tasks, from community-need submissions.
2. People bid on tasks, eBay-style; the best are released like gig tickets.
3. Clock in with the band.
4. The Academy reviews the job; points are released once it's confirmed complete. No deductions for time taken.

- Jobs are also allocated automatically every day, so everyone has baseline work, and everyone does **one automatic task a day**. No one is above doing their bit.
- **7–8am: the under-16s drop.** Community-focused, learning-based, points-heavy tasks.
- **9–10am: the adult market.** A daily scramble.
- Families compete as teams: more bands open, more jobs secured. One band per person.
- Task boards are hyperlocal, covering a few thousand people each.
- **The Director does a daily task too**, always in the learning section, with the kids.

### Housing

- A home is a right for every family. How it evolves, and what's inside, depends on points.

## The Secret

- The Great Reconnecting was not natural. The Director's family's global defence company ran an AI-run test of a new nuclear weapon designed to neutralise another bomb with zero detonation. With too little human control, the AI went rogue: two enormous bombs detonated at once under the ocean on opposite sides of the globe, shifting the tectonic plates.
- The cover story is accidentally true: it really was "out of human hands".
- The cover-up held because the undersea cables were severed (weeks of blackout), water absorbed much of the radiation, the company controlled satellite and sensing data, and suspicious scientists died, were absorbed into the Academy, were dismissed, or were moved to Trovata. Elevated readings are blamed on volcanic vents.
- The company still exists as the core of the new world system, reinvented by the Director. Its AI still runs bands, points and Exhibition profiles. The AI is not the villain: it's a mirror to whoever controls it. *(open: the company's name, old and new)*

## The two scars

- **Isola:** seabed that rose during the event; the first blast site's scar, cleaned up for show and turned into the Realignment island. Physical proof the event wasn't an accident, hidden in plain sight as a prison. A seabed jungle (coral cliffs, kelp hardened into trees, glowing fungi, whale bones and shipwreck hulls, marine snow, hydrothermal vents, brine pools) with soft, spa-like Realignment buildings. Arrivals find it beautiful and strangely free. People rarely return to where they came from, so rumours fill the gap. Italian for "island"; a nod to Nonna.
- **Trovata:** the second blast site, still contaminated. Officially just forgotten, uninhabitable volcanic land. In secret, where the Director sends people who know too much (truth-tellers, his sister) and people broken by testing. Some have built a free, off-grid life there, at a cost written in their bodies. To him it's a "messy drawer" he hasn't checked in years. Italian for "found".

## The true crime

- **The band is secretly a radiation exposure meter**, like a nuclear worker's dosimeter: it measures exposure, a number, never a diagnosis. It explains why everyone must wear one, at all times, from 2035.
- People whose readings climb too high are quietly removed to Realignment on Isola, where new fixes are tested on them. Those who respond well, or whose exposure was light, are returned to a different realm. Those broken by the tests are discarded to Trovata.
- No illness caused by the Reconnecting can be treated, because honest research would mean naming radiation, which would prove the blast. The lie blocks the science, and he chooses the lie. The illnesses have started seeping into the mainland ("the bleed").
- **The Exhibition's hidden purpose:** the Director is hunting for the young mind that can cure what he can't name. The curious, out-of-the-box thinkers he needs are exactly the people his system removes.
- **Recalibrate's real addition:** for the first time, bands quietly report where people are. It rolls out during the Grand Final, while the whole world is watching.
- **The Compass is secretly guided** by the AI and band data.
- The health machinery is never shown on the page: it stays off-page, like the Capitol's inner workings.

## The Director (full)

- From an old-money family that knew the truth. Father: the CEO, died in the event. Older brother: widely liked, running for US president, died in the event. Mother: still alive, barely, in her mid-90s; knows the truth and is complicit ("under the rug"). Sister: disappeared to Trovata. Aunt: see below.
- **Motive: atonement.** He believes telling people they ruined the world would cause more destruction. His silence heals the world, honours his mother's legacy, and protects his family. The noble lie.
- **Arc:** understanding turns into paranoid control: "this works, so it stays." The spare who became king; temporary powers that became permanent.
- **Blind spot:** he despised his father's business and doesn't know who Nonna was.
- **Decision log** (BE = Before Evil, PE = Post Evil): naming the show and the seat of government *The Academy* (BE); houses, crests, colours and the Academy year as the greatest show on Earth (BE); abolishing money, then introducing points (BE); dismantling nuclear weapons in public ceremonies (BE); the first Exhibition, 2037 (BE); Realignment as rehabilitation, championed by his aunt (BE); exiling truth-tellers to Trovata (PE); his sister's disappearance (PE); secretly testing on people with high exposure readings (PE); blocking honest research into the bleed (PE); Recalibrate and its location sharing (PE).
